## Brand Positioning Framework

### Positioning Statement Template

```
For [target segment] who [unmet need or job-to-be-done],
[brand] is the [category frame of reference] that [primary differentiating benefit].
Unlike [most-credible alternative], we [reason to believe / proof point].
```

A good positioning statement is mutually exclusive (rules competitors out), collectively exhaustive (covers the buyer's full need), and falsifiable (you could test the differentiator).

## E-E-A-T Checklist

Google's Experience-Expertise-Authoritativeness-Trustworthiness applies to every public-facing surface (site, ads, social, PR).

### Experience
- [ ] Real customer outcomes with named entities or anonymized but specific results
- [ ] Process / behind-the-scenes content showing actual operating reality
- [ ] Time-in-market signal (years founded, customers served, units shipped)
- [ ] First-person voice where author is named and bio is linkable

### Expertise
- [ ] Technical depth in pillar content (white paper / case study / methodology page)
- [ ] Author bios with credentials and traceable history
- [ ] Specialized vocabulary used correctly and explained for non-specialists
- [ ] Original research, data, or proprietary frameworks published

### Authoritativeness
- [ ] Earned media in recognized outlets (industry trade, mainstream, peer-reviewed)
- [ ] Inbound links from authoritative domains (DA 50+)
- [ ] Speaking/guest content (podcasts, panels, conferences)
- [ ] Reviews and ratings on trusted third-party platforms

### Trustworthiness
- [ ] Transparent pricing or clear "request a quote" path
- [ ] HTTPS, privacy policy, cookie consent (GDPR/CCPA), terms of service
- [ ] Verified business profile (Google Business, LinkedIn Company)
- [ ] Refund, guarantee, or SLA terms visible
- [ ] Regulated industries: license numbers, disclosures, fair-balance statements

## Pricing Strategy Frameworks

### Three-Tier (Good-Better-Best)
| Tier | Purpose | Psychology |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Volume + portfolio build | Charm pricing ($197, $297) |
| Standard | Margin sweet-spot, ~70% mix | Anchored by Premium |
| Premium | Anchor + halo | Round number, exclusivity cues |

### Value-Based Pricing
1. Price reflects buyer-perceived ROI, not cost-plus
2. Quantify outcome ("reduces support tickets by 40%")
3. Frame as investment vs alternative-cost
4. Tier by feature gates aligned to buyer segments
5. Never lead with price — lead with outcome

### Penetration vs Skimming
- **Penetration** (low price, fast share): network-effect products, marketplaces, commodity-adjacent DTC
- **Skimming** (high price, harvest then drop): premium launches, regulated novelty, luxury
- **Hybrid**: skimming on hero SKU + penetration on entry tier

## SEO Audit Checklist (Universal)

- [ ] Title tags <60 chars, unique, keyword-led
- [ ] Meta descriptions <155 chars with CTA
- [ ] H1 single per page, contains primary keyword
- [ ] Image alt text descriptive, keyword-aware, not stuffed
- [ ] Schema.org markup (Organization + Product/Service/Article)
- [ ] Mobile Core Web Vitals: LCP <2.5s, INP <200ms, CLS <0.1
- [ ] XML sitemap submitted; robots.txt sane
- [ ] HTTPS, canonical tags, hreflang where multi-region
- [ ] Internal linking depth <=3 clicks from home
- [ ] Backlink hygiene quarterly (disavow toxic)
- [ ] Local: NAP consistency, Google Business Profile, citations
- [ ] Content freshness signals on pillar pages

## Premium / Luxury Brand Principles

1. **Exclusivity**: gated access, by-application, member-only drops
2. **Storytelling**: provenance, creator intent, named artisan
3. **Quality signals**: materials, certifications, heritage
4. **Scarcity**: numbered editions, capacity caps, waitlists
5. **Packaging is product**: unboxing as ritual, white-glove logistics
6. **Price as quality signal**: under-pricing destroys perceived value
7. **Restraint in promotion**: never discount the hero; create access tiers instead
